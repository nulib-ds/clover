## What you can ask

Ask in your own words, as you would ask a colleague. Your agent finds what these pages
offer it by itself.

**"What would show my film, with its captions?"** Your agent finds the parts of Clover
that handle what you describe, what each one can do, and its page in these docs. Give it
the address of your IIIF resource, and it looks at what the resource has (images, sound,
video, 3D, annotations, a search service) and suggests a layout that fits.

**"Write that as web components."** It writes the layout's code, in React or as web
components, ready to copy, and tells you how to install Clover. If something you asked
for won't work, such as a part Clover doesn't have or a setting no part takes, it says
so and suggests what will.

**"Show me."** It draws the layout here, live, over your resource or one of these docs'
examples, with its code under it. On a component's page, ask to see the component, and
it shows you that one.

## What happens on the page

The preview takes the place of the page's first example, with its code under it in React
or as web components, as you chose at the top of the page. Press "Show this page's
example" to bring the page's own back. Your place on the page and your keyboard focus
stay where they are: the preview comes into view only when you ask to see it. A screen
reader announces it as it appears and as it goes.

Leaving the page takes the preview with it. Pages without an example, such as Get
started and Styling, have nowhere to show one: ask on this page,
[Composing](/docs/composing), or any component's page.

## What it reads, and never sends

- **What your resource is, not what it says.** To see what your resource has, it looks
  at what kind of material it is and what Clover could do with it. Its titles,
  descriptions, metadata and transcripts stay out of what it tells your agent.
- **Nothing of yours.** It reads your resource as anyone on the web could: without your
  sign-in cookies, and without the address of the page you're reading.
- **About Clover only.** What it tells your agent is about Clover: its parts, their
  settings, their code and these docs. It never tells your agent about the material in
  these docs' examples.
- **This page only.** A preview changes nothing but the page you're on, and only until
  you bring its example back or leave.

## What you need

An AI agent that works in your browser and uses WebMCP, a way for web pages to offer
agents tools of their own. WebMCP is a
[draft from the W3C](https://webmachinelearning.github.io/webmcp/), and browsers are
starting to try it.

Where your browser doesn't offer it yet, nothing on these pages changes, and your agent
can still read them: every page is also Markdown, at its address with `.md` added, and
[llms.txt](/llms.txt) lists them all.

Working on Clover itself?
[WEBMCP.md](https://github.com/nulib-ds/clover/blob/main/WEBMCP.md) explains how these
tools are built and how they grow with Clover.
