## Stylesheets

| Stylesheet                                                 | What it gives                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `base.css`                                                 | Clover's structure alone: what shows, where each box sits, how large it is |
| `base.css`, then `spacing.css`                             | Its structure, and its margins, paddings and gaps                          |
| `styles.css`, and `templates.css` for Document and Gallery | Its whole look too: colors, edges, corners, shadows, type and motion       |

Clover's stylesheets sit in `@layer clover`, so your CSS wins without a fight over
specificity. The first two leave the look to you: see [Base styling](#base-styling).

A recording, a Scene, a map and a vertical slider load their styles with their code, so a
page without them loads none. With `base.css`, they load none of Clover's.

<div data-code-flavor="react">

A map brings MapLibre's stylesheet either way, in a layer after Clover's, unless your page
imports `maplibre-gl/dist/maplibre-gl.css` itself.

Import `styles.css` once, and `templates.css` for Document and Gallery. With no
stylesheet, every component still renders a plain, usable layout.

To load less, import only your components' sheets, in place of `styles.css` and never
beside it: `styles/core.css` first, then the sheet of each component on the page or inside
one (Collection holds `Items`). A page of images with `Root`, `Status`, `Body`, `Position`
and `Items` needs two:

```jsx react
import "@nulib/clover/styles/core.css";
import "@nulib/clover/styles/items.css";
```

| Sheet                          | Components                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `styles/core.css`              | `Root`, `Body` and `Canvas`, `Status`, `Label`, the Primitives                                                              |
| `styles/items.css`             | `Items` and `Position`, and the items in `Collection` and the Panel's Views section                                         |
| `styles/items-vertical.css`    | `Items` as a vertical slider, as the `Default` template shows it for a Manifest read down the page                          |
| `styles/panel.css`             | `Panel`, and `About`, `Annotations`, `Collection`, `Contents`, `Download`, `Search` and `Transcript`, in the Panel or alone |
| `styles/media.css`             | Video, sound, `Timeline` and animations, in a `Body` or a `Canvas` too                                                      |
| `styles/scene.css`             | `Scene`, and a 3D model in a `Body`                                                                                         |
| `styles/map.css`               | `Map`, the Panel's Map section, and the map a Canvas opens for a place                                                      |
| `styles/split.css`             | `Split`                                                                                                                     |
| `styles/collection-select.css` | `CollectionSelect`                                                                                                          |
| `styles/default.css`           | The `Default` template, and `PanelPane`, the information panel every template shares                                        |

For Document or Gallery, add `styles/default.css` and `templates.css`. The sheets that load
with their code (`media`, `scene`, `map`, `items-vertical`) need importing only where a
server renders their component, to style its first paint.

</div>

<div data-code-flavor="web-components">

The elements bring Clover's whole look themselves: each adds its styles once, first in
`<head>`, so your rules still win. A map brings MapLibre's stylesheet as MapLibre ships
it, outside any layer, even where your page has its own.

</div>

## Base styling

For a look of your own, use `base.css` in place of Clover's styles. It keeps the structure
of every component and template (what shows, where each box sits, what lies over what,
how large each box and target is, and the cursor over what you can press or drag) and
defines the scale's [custom properties](/docs/styling/variables#sizes-and-spaces). Add
`spacing.css` after it to keep [Clover's spacing](/docs/styling/spacing), or write your
own.

<div data-code-flavor="react">

Import them in place of `styles.css` and `templates.css`, then your own CSS. They serve
the Viewer in every template.

</div>

<div data-code-flavor="web-components">

Link them before the elements' script, then your own CSS. The elements find `base.css` as
they load and bring none of Clover's styles. If it comes later, they keep Clover's rather
than restyle what's already on the page. With a bundler, import
`@nulib/clover/base.css` before `@nulib/clover/web-components`.

</div>

### A layout that works

`base.css` alone gives you a layout that works:

- Whatever lies over other content (a control's name, a menu, a sticky heading, the Panel
  over a narrow page) gets a surface in your page's colors,
  <span data-code-flavor="both">`Canvas` and `CanvasText`</span>, until your
  look replaces it.
- A control Clover's look restyles, such as a language's code or the eye that hides a set,
  stays the browser's radio or checkbox, focus ring and all.
- Margins and paddings that hold the layout stay: an `auto` margin that aligns a box, the
  room PanelToggle keeps in its corner, and Items' zero margins, which undo the 40px a
  browser puts on each side of a figure.
- A hotspot over the picture stays see-through.
- SplitHandle, the bar you drag between the panes to resize the Panel, shows from top to
  bottom in your text's color.

Your look decides colors and focus. Check its colors in the
[contrast guide](/docs/styling/colors#contrast-guide), keep a focus ring on every
control, and keep every target at least 24px.
