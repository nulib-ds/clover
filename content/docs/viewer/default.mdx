## How it is put together

`Default` is what the Viewer draws when you give it no `template`: Clover 3.x's Viewer,
rebuilt. It is one React component of about 300 lines and one stylesheet, and it uses
nothing private. Every piece is a part `@nulib/clover` exports, or one of four small
hooks beside it in `templates/shared/`. Anything it does, a layout of yours can do.

It has five regions. The header, the stage and the strip run down a main pane; the panel
has a pane of its own beside it, and the bar between them shares the width.

<DefaultAnatomy />

1. **The header**: the resource's thumbnail and title.
2. **The stage**: the view itself (an image to zoom, a spread, video or sound), with its
   controls, and the panel's toggle while the panel is closed.
3. **The strip**: where the reader is, as "Page 3 · Page 4" and "3 / 17", and the
   thumbnails.
4. **The bar**: drag it, click it or use the arrow keys on it to share the width.
5. **The panel**: About, and whichever of Collection, Contents, Annotations, Search,
   Transcript and Map the resource has.

In outline, simplified, the markup is:

```jsx
<Clover.Root className="clover-template-default">
  <Clover.Split className="clover-template-default-body">
    <Clover.SplitPane className="clover-template-default-main">
      <header className="clover-template-default-header">
        <Clover.Thumbnail />
        <Clover.Label as="h2" />
      </header>
      <Clover.Content className="clover-template-default-stage" />
      <div className="clover-template-default-rail">
        <ViewTitles />
        <Clover.Position />
        <Clover.Items layout="carousel" />
      </div>
    </Clover.SplitPane>
    <Clover.SplitHandle />
    <Clover.SplitPane name="panel" size="24rem">
      <Clover.Panel />
    </Clover.SplitPane>
  </Clover.Split>
</Clover.Root>
```

The rest of this page walks through the real code, region by region, then gives the
whole of it.

## The root and the split

Everything sits in one `Clover.Root`, which loads the resource and shares it with every
part inside, wherever it is placed. The root carries the template's class, and
`data-fill` when `fill` is set. Past `h6`, it is the group its title names
([The header](#the-header)).

Inside it, a `Clover.Split` lays out the main pane and the panel's pane side by side,
with the bar between them. It remembers the width the reader chooses, under
`storageKey`. It also says where the panel is, in `data-panel` and `data-panel-open`, for
the stylesheet. Escape anywhere in it puts a floating panel away.

## Beside or over

The panel sits beside the stage where there is room for both, and slides over it where
there is not. Default measures its own width, not the window's, so a Viewer in a narrow
column of a wide page behaves as it would on a phone. The stage keeps at least 16rem and
the panel 14rem; under that and the bar, 31rem, the panel goes over.

## The header

The resource's thumbnail and title, unless `header={false}`. The thumbnail is
decorative (`alt=""`), since the title beside it says what it shows. The title is a
heading at `headingLevel`, an `h2` by default. Past `h6`, where HTML's headings end, it
is no heading: `Clover.titled` makes it a paragraph that names the Viewer instead, as
every part does with a title that would be an `h7`.

## The stage

`Clover.Content` is the stage. It draws whatever the view is (an image to zoom, a
spread, video or sound with its transport, an animation) with the controls over it. Its
children are drawn among those controls, which is where the panel's toggle goes while
the panel is not docked beside it. `Clover.Status` tells screen readers the resource is
loading, or why it failed; the stage shows it, so nothing on the page moves.

<SourceFile
  file="templates/default/Default.tsx"
  from="<Clover.Status"
  to="</Clover.Content>"
/>

The stage is `--clover-content-height` tall, `min(65vh, 40rem)` by default. With
`fill`, it takes what the header and the strip leave in the box instead, never less than
16rem.

## The strip

Under the stage: where the reader is, and the thumbnails. `Clover.Position` is the
count, "3 / 17". `Clover.Items` draws the thumbnails, as a carousel by default (`items`
picks another layout, or none), and its arrows are Previous and Next. A resource of one
view has no strip at all.

## The bar

`Clover.SplitHandle` is the bar between the panes, drawn only while the panel is docked.
Drag it by its grip; click or tap it to step the panel from its narrowest to 24rem to
its widest and round again; or focus it and use the arrow keys, Home and End for the
limits. No step needs a drag.

<SourceFile
  file="templates/default/Default.tsx"
  from="{docked && <Clover.SplitHandle"
  toLine=")}"
/>

Right after the bar sits the panel's toggle, on the bar itself, its spine. `useSpine`
lays it across the bar, level with the stage's controls, and measures again as the panes
resize: the bar dragged, a title wrapping. It is not inside the handle, a `separator`,
whose contents assistive technology does not reach.

## The panel

The panel's pane is 24rem wide to start, and at least 14rem. `Clover.Panel` lists the
resource's sections, stacked as an accordion or, with `panelLayout="tabs"`, as tabs. Its
headings are a level below the title, or at `headingLevel` with no header, where they
take the title's place in the page's outline. Docked, the toggle closes it; over the
stage, it carries its own close button and closes on Escape.

### Over the stage

On a phone the panel is a drawer. `useSlide` slides it in and out, and hides it once it
is out of sight. `useFocusOnOpen` takes focus into it as it opens, as an overlay should.
`useDragToClose` lets a finger drag it back off the way it came. A scrim covers the stage
under it, and a strip of the page stays uncovered: a click there, Escape, or the panel's
close button puts it away, and focus in it goes back to the toggle.

## Styles

Default's styles are in `@nulib/clover/styles.css`, with the parts', so a Viewer with no
template needs one stylesheet. Every class starts with `clover-template-default`. Each
region is `--clover-inset` inside all round, so nothing around it needs a margin.

| Class                                 | What it styles                                                               |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `.clover-template-default`            | The root: a column, and the stage's height, `--clover-content-height`.       |
| `.clover-template-default[data-fill]` | With `fill`: as tall as its box, the stage taking what the rest leave.       |
| `.clover-template-default-header`     | The header: the thumbnail and the title, `s` apart.                          |
| `.clover-template-default-thumbnail`  | The thumbnail, `xl` high, at its own shape up to 5rem wide.                  |
| `.clover-template-default-title`      | The title, at the large text step.                                           |
| `.clover-template-default-body`       | The split, with `data-panel` (`"beside"` or `"over"`) and `data-panel-open`. |
| `.clover-template-default-main`       | The main pane: the header, the stage and the strip, in a column.             |
| `.clover-template-default-stage`      | The stage, `Clover.Content`.                                                 |
| `.clover-template-default-rail`       | The strip; `-rail-header` holds `-titles` and the position.                  |
| `.clover-template-default-spine`      | The toggle on the bar.                                                       |
| `.clover-template-default-panel`      | The panel's pane: beside, it scrolls within the main pane's height.          |
| `.clover-template-default-scrim`      | The wash over the stage under a floating panel.                              |

Beside the stage, the panel's pane is as tall as the main pane, however long its
sections run: size containment keeps them from making the row taller, and it scrolls
within.

## Accessibility

Default meets WCAG 2.2 AA, with the parts it is made of. What is left is checking, not
code: on phones, in screen readers, and with native speakers of its translations, as
[ACCESSIBILITY.md](https://github.com/nulib-ds/clover/blob/main/ACCESSIBILITY.md) lists.
What Default adds as a layout:

- **Headings that fit the page.** The title is at `headingLevel` and the panel's
  sections a level below, so a screen reader's list of headings matches the page as it
  looks. None is ever past `h6`: there, a title names what it heads instead.
- **Focus that follows.** The toggle moves as it is pressed, and focus goes with it. A
  floating panel takes focus as it opens and gives it back to the toggle as it closes;
  one that closes as the Viewer narrows hands focus back first.
- **No step needs a drag.** The bar steps on a click or a tap, and moves with the arrow
  keys; a drawer dragged closed also closes from its button, Escape, or a click beside it.
- **Nothing covered for good.** A floating panel always leaves a strip of the page in
  sight, and Escape anywhere in the Viewer puts it away.
- **Zoom and text spacing.** It measures its own width, so at 400% zoom it lays out as on
  a phone. Titles wrap rather than being cut short.
- **Reduced motion.** The panel and its scrim appear and go without sliding or fading.

## Make it yours

Copy it, and change what you like:

1. Copy `Default.tsx` and `Default.css` from `packages/clover/src/templates/default/`
   into your app, with the four hooks it imports from `templates/shared/`: `useSlide.ts`,
   `useSpine.ts`, `useFollowFocus.ts` and `useDragToClose.ts`.
2. Import the parts from `@nulib/clover` where it imports `../../parts`.
3. Rename it, and its classes from `clover-template-default` to your own, so its styles
   and Clover's never meet.
4. Pass it as the Viewer's `template`. A template is a component that takes the Root's
   props, and the Viewer hands it everything but `template`.

```jsx
import Viewer from "@nulib/clover";
import { Catalog } from "./Catalog";
import "@nulib/clover/styles.css";
import "./Catalog.css";

<Viewer iiifContent={url} template={Catalog} />;
```

The parts read the Root's state wherever they sit, so moving one is moving its markup:
to put the strip over the stage, move its `div` above `Clover.Content`. Moving the panel
to the other side takes more, since the bar and the toggle go with it and the drawer
slides from that side (`useDragToClose`'s `side`, and the stylesheet's
`inset-inline-end`). For a layout that starts from nothing, see
[Composing layouts](/docs/composing).
