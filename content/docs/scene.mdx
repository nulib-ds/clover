## API reference

Draws the Presentation 4 Scene you hand it, or loads one itself (`iiifContent`: a Manifest
gives its first Scene). Inside a `Root`, given nothing, it draws the Root's view when that
is a Scene; [`Body`](/docs/body) draws a Scene view with it, unless a renderer of yours
claims it. It fills its parent, so give the parent a height.

What draws the models, Clover's own renderer (or three.js, `engine="three"`, which a page
opts into), loads the first time a Scene comes near the window.

A drag turns the models and a pinch zooms. The controls sit together in the lower right,
over a soft shade, and show while the pointer moves over the view or keyboard focus is in
it: zoom and full screen in a row, then the rotate pad. Drag the pad's disc to turn the
models as you would drag the view; the globe on it turns with them and shows where their
front is. Each arrow turns them the way it points, a step for a press, or on and on while
you hold it, and Reset view, in the pad's corner, brings them home. With the view focused,
the arrow keys turn the models (with Shift, further), R and Shift+R turn them right and
left, plus and minus zoom, and 0 resets. A plain wheel scrolls the page.

### Customizing engine

Clover draws a Scene with its own engine, `clover`, by default, for its size. Each engine
loads the first time a Scene comes near the window:

- `engine="clover"`, Clover's own WebGL2 renderer: <Measured size="sceneCloverOnDemandGzip" /> gzipped.
- `engine="three"`, three.js: <Measured size="sceneOnDemandGzip" /> gzipped, for what
  Clover's does not draw yet.

Choose three.js for what Clover's engine does not draw yet: glTF's material extensions
beyond unlit, such as clearcoat, transmission and sheen, skins and morph targets (at
rest), sparse accessors and a second set of texture coordinates. Clover's engine says
which of these a model needs, in an alert, rather than drawing it wrong. Neither engine
draws compressed models (Draco, Meshopt, KTX2) or plays animations yet.

three.js is opt-in: install it, and register it once, before a Scene draws. A page that
never does carries none of it.

<div data-code-flavor="react">

```js
// npm install three
import { registerThreeEngine } from "@nulib/clover/three";

registerThreeEngine();
```

</div>

`engine` differs.

### Scene or Body

In a layout, place [`Body`](/docs/body). It handles every container the IIIF Presentation
API defines, a Canvas, a Timeline or a Scene, so as your reader moves from one view to the
next, it switches between Canvas, Timeline and Scene on the fly. It adds a height, your
renderers and the loading mark too.

```jsx
<Clover.Root iiifContent="https://iiif.io/api/cookbook/recipe/0608-mvm-3d/v4/manifest.json">
  <Clover.Body />
</Clover.Root>
```

## Formats

Scene draws glTF 2.0, the open format for 3D on the web that the IIIF Cookbook's Scenes
paint, as binary (`.glb`, `model/gltf-binary`) or JSON (`.gltf`, `model/gltf+json`), with
its materials (metal and roughness), textures and node hierarchies. A model in any other
format shows an alert saying why, never a broken view.

The IIIF Cookbook's [0608, Simplest Manifest: 3D](https://iiif.io/api/cookbook/recipe/0608-mvm-3d/)
draws as it asks: one model at the Scene's origin, lit and framed by Clover, since the
Scene names no camera or light. Models placed by a `PointSelector` on their target, and
turned, scaled and moved by the transforms on their `SpecificResource`, in order, are
drawn too.

## Accessibility

The view is announced as a rotatable 3D model, named by the Scene's label, and its
description lists its keys. Everything a drag or a pinch does, a key or a button does
too, so the models turn and zoom without a pointer.

The controls are named buttons, each with its key in its popover and at least 24px across,
in the order up, left, right, down, Reset view; on a small view they shrink, but never
under 24px. The pad's disc is a convenience for a mouse or a finger, so screen readers
skip it: its arrows and the view's keys do everything it does. The controls stay on screen
while keyboard focus is in them. Nothing moves on its own, and a model that cannot be
drawn is explained in an alert.
