# Canvas

<Callout type="warning">
  **Experimental.** `Canvas` is Clover's own 2D renderer, in place of the
  OpenSeadragon and Vidstack that Clover 3.x draws with. Its API may change in
  any release.
</Callout>

A pan and zoom surface for IIIF images, video and sound, drawn with WebGL2 and falling
back to Canvas2D. Everything on a IIIF Canvas shares one camera, so it moves together.

### Annotations

Rectangular (`xywh`) annotation targets become hotspots, as in `Image`: hover or focus
one to see its label, in a popover that <kbd>Escape</kbd> hides, and click it (or press
the <kbd>Enter</kbd> key) to zoom to it. Pressing <kbd>Tab</kbd> reaches them in the
order the annotations are given, and moves the picture, no further than needed, to bring
the one it reaches out from under the controls. The comment control in the cluster lists
them by their text; picking one zooms to it and keeps its hotspot highlighted and marked
as current.

### Placeholder Canvas

A Canvas with a `placeholderContainer` (Presentation 3's `placeholderCanvas`) shows that
Canvas's image first, fitted to the frame and centred, as the Viewer does. The Canvas
itself is held back until you open it from the toggle in the top right, or by clicking
the image: nothing is fetched, and there is no pan or zoom, no annotations or `Choice`,
and no video or sound until then. The information control stays available. The toggle
then closes the Canvas again, back to its placeholder. Open or closed holds as the
Canvases change, so a reader paging through a book opens it once; a video or sound
starts at its poster each time, since opening it plays it. Here the Canvases of
[Cookbook 0029](https://iiif.io/api/cookbook/recipe/0029-metadata-anywhere/) are given

## IIIF Canvases

`Canvas` draws IIIF Canvases: pass one, or the few you want shown together, as
`canvases`. Each Canvas's painting annotations are composed at their targets, and
several Canvases are laid out in reading order by `viewingDirection`.

`Canvas` knows nothing of the Manifest around those Canvases. Deciding what to show —
reading `behavior` to group a Manifest into single pages, spreads or one joined object —
and stepping between views is the host's job, as it is the Viewer's. Each example below
is a small host doing exactly that for a IIIF Cookbook recipe, with its previous / next
below the `Canvas` rather than inside it. The grouping rules it uses are Clover's own
(`groupViews`, exported from `@nulib/clover`), the same ones the Viewer follows.

#### Styling captions

Clover's captions are sized on its scale, from the page's own text size, so a reader's
larger default text or zoom makes them larger too. They grow a step at a time as the
stage widens. A system's own caption settings (size, colour, background) reach the
captions where the system's player shows them: an iPhone's full screen and picture in
picture. On the page, set them yourself:

| Custom property                   | Default            | What it sets                                            |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--clover-player-caption-surface` | `rgb(0 0 0 / 85%)` | The box behind each cue.                                |
| `--clover-player-caption-text`    | `#fff`             | The cue's text.                                         |
| `--clover-player-caption-edge`    | `none`             | A `text-shadow` round the text: an outline or a shadow. |

Keep the text at 4.5:1 against the box, as the defaults are (WCAG 1.4.3). Size and font
are your own CSS on the cue, which wins over Clover's layer:

```css
.my-player {
  --clover-player-caption-surface: rgb(0 0 0 / 60%);
  --clover-player-caption-edge: 0 0 2px #000, 0 0 2px #000;
}

.my-player .clover-canvas-captions-cue {
  font-size: 1.25rem;
  font-family: "Atkinson Hyperlegible", sans-serif;
}
```

## Features

`Canvas` covers what `Image` does, apart from OpenSeadragon's own configuration:

- **IIIF Image API 3.0, 2.1.1 and 2.0.** Each service is tiled from its `info.json`:
  - from its `tiles` when it lists them
  - from its `sizes` when it is a level 0 tree that lists only sizes
  - from tiles Clover cuts itself when it is level 1 or 2 and lists none

  Requests use each version's canonical spelling, which is all a static level 0 tree
  answers to. A body whose service cannot be reached falls back to its `id`.

- **Lazy loading.** Nothing is fetched until a Canvas comes within half a screen of
  view: no `info.json` or tiles, and no video, sound or captions. A page of many Canvases
  costs nothing until it is scrolled, and a hidden one waits until it is shown.
- **Deep zoom.** It loads the coarsest level first, then the level that fits the screen,
  nearest the centre first. While the view moves it fetches for where it will land, and
  tiles that scroll out of view are cancelled. New tiles reach the GPU a few per frame, so
  a burst of them never stalls one. Tiles stay cached up to a memory budget.
- **Several images** (`body` or `src` arrays) are laid out side by side at a common
  height, and **`body.region`** (`x,y,w,h` or `pct:x,y,w,h`) shows only that part of an
  image.
- **Annotations** with rectangular targets become hotspots.
- **Controls:** the same cluster as `Image`, including `controlButtons` replacements.
- **Full screen and rotation**, and a **navigator** (an overview, off by default:
  `navigator` turns it on).
- **An annotations menu**, from a comment control, when there are annotations.
- **A caption** of each Canvas's `summary` and `metadata`, from an information control.
- **A placeholder Canvas** is shown fitted in place of the Canvas until it is opened.
- **No CORS needed.** Images served without CORS are still shown: WebGL may not read
  them, so they are placed as real `<img>` elements by the same camera.
- **Video and sound** (`Video` and `Sound` painting bodies) play in Clover's own
  transport, with WebVTT captions, a placeholder poster, and an accompanying image for
  sound. HLS plays through `hls.js` wherever the browser supports it, and natively
  elsewhere.

## Interaction

The controls, navigator, a video or sound's transport, and a soft scrim behind them appear
while the mouse moves over the Canvas or focus is anywhere inside it. They fade after
`hideDelay`, or a second after the mouse or keyboard focus leaves, as the Viewer's
Player does. Chrome shown by keyboard focus stays while focus is inside, and a control
stays while the mouse rests on it. A finger, or a pen that does not hover, has no timer:
a tap on the picture shows the chrome, and the next tap hides it, however long the reader
takes. A pen that hovers (a desktop tablet's, say) points as a mouse does. A press where
faded chrome sits only shows it: nothing beneath, a hotspot included, takes that press.
Focus that no press brought (the keyboard's, or a screen reader's) holds the chrome until
it leaves.

| Input                       | Action                                                    |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Drag                        | Pan, with momentum on release                             |
| Click                       | Zoom in (<kbd>Shift</kbd>-click: out); at the limit, home |
| Pinch                       | Zoom about the pinch                                      |
| Trackpad pinch              | Zoom about the pointer                                    |
| Wheel                       | Scrolls the page, unless `scrollToZoom`                   |
| Tap (touch, pen)            | Show or hide the controls                                 |
| Double tap (touch, pen)     | Zoom in; at the limit, home                               |
| Arrow keys                  | Pan (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: further)                           |
| <kbd>+</kbd> / <kbd>-</kbd> | Zoom in / out                                             |
| <kbd>0</kbd>                | Home, upright                                             |
| <kbd>r</kbd> / <kbd>R</kbd> | Rotate 90° clockwise / anticlockwise                      |
| Navigator                   | Press or drag to move there                               |

Zoomed in as far as it goes, a click or a double tap goes home, so one pointer can always
zoom back out, even with the zoom and reset controls turned off (WCAG 2.5.1). Turn off the
click zooms (`gestures`) as well, and pinch is the only zoom left on touch: give the reader
one of your own through the handle (`home()`, `zoomBy(0.5)`).

The viewport tells assistive technology that it can be operated. It keeps its `img` role
(a `group` when it holds hotspots), described as a "zoomable image", and names its keys in
`aria-keyshortcuts` and, in words, in its description. A lone video or sound names the
player's keys instead, and a Canvas the host holds still (`navigable: false`) names no
camera keys. Where Clover's strings are not in the page's language, the role
keeps its own name, since an attribute cannot carry a language.

A single video or sound Canvas is a player rather than a picture to explore. It is always
fitted to the stage, at the video's own aspect, and does not pan, zoom or rotate. Its
only chrome is the transport at the bottom, which carries full screen; there are no top
controls or scrim, unless it has a `Choice` to pick or information to read. A click on the picture plays and pauses, and
the focused Canvas takes these keys:

| Input                           | Action                     |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| <kbd>Space</kbd> / <kbd>k</kbd> | Play / pause               |
| <kbd>j</kbd> / <kbd>l</kbd>     | Back / forward ten seconds |
| <kbd>m</kbd>                    | Mute                       |

## Usage

`Canvas` ships in `@nulib/clover`, the 4.x package, which is not published yet. Import
its stylesheet once, anywhere in the app.

```jsx
import * as Clover from "@nulib/clover";
import "@nulib/clover/styles.css";

<Clover.Canvas
  src="https://iiif.io/api/image/3.0/example/reference/918ecd18c2592080851777620de9bcb5-gottingen"
  isTiledImage
  label="Göttingen market square"
  onReady={(canvas) => canvas.zoomBy(2)}
  onViewportChange={(bounds) => console.log(bounds)}
/>;
```

At home the picture fills the stage edge to edge, and its placeholder the same, so the
still hands over to the Canvas without moving. Set `--clover-canvas-inset` to keep a
margin clear round it at home (zoomed in, it still runs to the edges). The controls sit
`--clover-canvas-chrome-inset` in from the edges, the region's inset (`--clover-inset`,
`1.618rem`) by default:

```css
.my-stage .clover-canvas {
  --clover-canvas-inset: var(--clover-inset);
}
```

## Props

`Canvas` reads IIIF Presentation 4, Clover's one data shape. Upgrade Presentation 2 or 3
first with `toPresentation4()`, which loads `@iiif/parser`'s upgrader on demand:

```jsx
import { Canvas, toPresentation4 } from "@nulib/clover";

const manifest = await toPresentation4(await (await fetch(url)).json());
<Canvas canvases={[manifest.items[0]]} />;
```

Inside a `Clover.Root`, leave out `canvases`, `body` and `src`: the Canvas shows the
Root's current view, and the Root does the loading and upgrading. See
[Viewer](/docs/viewer).

| Prop                 | Type                                                                       | Description                                                                        |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `canvases`           | Presentation 4 Canvases or Timelines                                       | One, or several shown together (a spread). See below.                              |
| `viewingDirection`   | `"left-to-right"` and so on                                                | How to lay out several `canvases`.                                                 |
| `body`               | IIIF painting body, or an array                                            | Image resources to draw. Takes precedence over `src`.                              |
| `src`                | `string \| string[]`                                                       | Image URLs, or image service URIs with `isTiledImage`.                             |
| `isTiledImage`       | `boolean`                                                                  | Treat each `src` as an image service (base URI or `info.json`).                    |
| `label`              | `InternationalString \| string`                                            | Accessible name for the viewport.                                                  |
| `language`           | `string`                                                                   | The language to read labels, Choices and annotations in. In a `Root`, the Root's.  |
| `instanceId`         | `string`                                                                   | A new id remounts the renderer.                                                    |
| `manifestId`         | `string`                                                                   | The id of the Manifest the Canvases are from. A new one clears the Canvas at once. |
| `annotations`        | `{ annotation, targetIndex }[]`                                            | Hotspots, as `Image` takes them. Rectangular targets only.                         |
| `onAnnotationActive` | `(id: string \| null) => void`                                             | The hovered or focused hotspot.                                                    |
| `navigator`          | `boolean`                                                                  | Show the overview in the top left. Default `false`.                                |
| `controls`           | `boolean \| { zoom, fullPage, rotation, reset, annotations, information }` | Which controls to show. Default all. See Interaction for zooming out without them. |
| `controlButtons`     | `ControlButtons`                                                           | Replace any control, as the Viewer's `controlButtons`.                             |
| `options`            | `CloverCanvasOptions`                                                      | See below.                                                                         |
| `onReady`            | `(handle: CanvasHandle) => void`                                           | Called once the first images are laid out and fitted.                              |
| `onViewportChange`   | `(bounds: Rect) => void`                                                   | The visible region, in image coordinates, at most once per frame.                  |
| `onMediaElement`     | `(element: HTMLVideoElement \| null) => void`                              | A time-based Canvas's media element, for a host's own transcript.                  |
| `onEnded`            | `() => void`                                                               | Playback reached the end: a host's cue to advance.                                 |
| `chapters`           | `{ label, lang?, start, end? }[]`                                          | Chapters of the video or sound, on the seek bar and in a menu, labels in `lang`.   |

### `options`

| -------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

### `CanvasHandle`

Coordinates are world units, which are the first image's own coordinates (its declared
`width`/`height`). That makes `getBounds()` an `xywh=` region as it stands.

`fitBounds(rect)`, `fitItemRect(index, rect)`, `itemRectToWorld(index, rect)`,
`fitCanvasRect(canvasId, rect)` (a region of one shown Canvas), `getCanvasRegion()` (the
visible part of the Canvas filling most of the view, in its own coordinates),
`getBounds()`, `getHomeBounds()`, `home()`, `zoomBy(factor, at?)`, `panBy(dx, dy)`,
`setRotation(degrees)`, `rotateBy(degrees)`, `getRotation()`, `getCamera()`,
`worldToScreenPoint(point)`, `screenToWorldPoint(point)`, and `on(event, listener)` for
`"viewport"`, `"change"`, `"error"` and `"backend"`.
